XVI Portuguese Category Seminar · Abstract

Sheaf representation of monoidal categories, revisited

Rui Soares Barbosa

INL – International Iberian Nanotechnology Laboratory, Portugal

Friday, 13 November · 15:00–15:30Sala Pedro NunesDownload abstract (PDF)

Every small monoidal category with universal finite joins of central idempotents is monoidally equivalent to the category of global sections of a sheaf of local monoidal categories (joint work with Chris Heunen [1]). The base space is extracted from the central idempotents of the monoidal structure itself, much like the Pierce spectrum of a ring [2]. The theorem extends the sheaf representation of toposes due to Lambek and Moerdijk [3] (see also Awodey [4]) and Stone’s representation of Boolean algebras [5] from the cartesian to the monoidal setting. For Hilbert modules, it relates to Takahashi’s representation by continuous fields of Hilbert spaces [6], whose fibres are quotients of our stalks, much as the residue field (fibre) is obtained from a local ring (stalk).

I will present this result and sketch more recent developments, in particular a version for closed categories [7]. There, one can work with the idempotent monads [U,−][U,-] instead of the central idempotents UU themselves. Meets and joins of these monads always exist, given by composites and pullbacks, so one can do away with the universal joins hypothesis. Locality of the stalks is again a disjunction property, now for a Russell–Prawitz-like disjunction defined from implication [8, 9]. Implicative semilattices [10] give an example beyond the scope of the original theorem. Finally, I will briefly outline possible connections to substructural logics, where central idempotents behave like formulas admitting weakening and an invertible contraction, such as the pure assertions of intuitionistic separation logic [11, 12], and to programming languages, where monads of this kind model security levels in information-flow type systems [13].

Keywords: Monoidal categories; closed categories; sheaf representation; central idempotents

Subject area: Monoidal categories, sheaves, and categorical logic

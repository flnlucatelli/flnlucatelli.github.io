XVI Portuguese Category Seminar · Abstract

How protomodular is your favourite category?

Maria Manuel Clementino1, Diana Rodelo2

1Department of Mathematics, University of Coimbra, Portugal
2Department of Mathematics, University of the Algarve, Portugal

Presenting author: Diana Rodelo

Friday, 13 November · 10:15–11:00Sala Pedro NunesDownload abstract (PDF)

The aim of this talk is to investigate a relative notion of protomodularity. We say that a finitely complete category 𝖢\mathsf{C} satisfies the ℳ\mathscr{M}-version of protomodularity when the change-of-base functors (of the fibration of points) are conservative with respect to a class ℳ\mathscr{M} of morphisms of 𝖢\mathsf{C}. When ℳ\mathscr{M} is the class of all (mono)morphisms of 𝖢\mathsf{C}, this notion is precisely that of an “ordinary” protomodular category. We analyse which conditions on ℳ\mathscr{M} are needed to obtain the relative ℳ\mathscr{M}-versions of some well-known properties of protomodular categories, such as the Split Short Five Lemma (when 𝖢\mathsf{C} is also pointed) and the relation with Mal’tsev categories. We then investigate the ℳ\mathscr{M}-version of protomodularity for different subclasses of (mono)morphisms in 𝖢\mathsf{C} such as: isomorphisms, strong monomorphisms, split monomorphisms, protosplit monomorphisms (i.e. kernels of split epimorphisms) and fully faithful monomorphisms in an 𝖮𝗋𝖽\mathsf{Ord}-enriched context. This leads to diverse examples of categories satisfying the ℳ\mathscr{M}-version of protomodularity for different choices of ℳ\mathscr{M}. None of these examples are protomodular categories in the usual sense, yet each retains a protomodular “flavour”, depending on the choice of ℳ\mathscr{M}.

Why Existence Splits: Prawitz's Ecumenical Logic and the Double-Negation Subtopos

Idir Maguenine

Université Paris Cité, France

Thursday, 12 November · 16:30–16:50Sala Pedro NunesDownload abstract (PDF)

In Prawitz’s ecumenical system [6], [7], a classical and an intuitionistic logician share conjunction, the universal quantifier, negation and absurdity, but each keeps their own disjunction, implication and existential quantifier (∨c,→c,∃c\vee_c,\to_c,\exists_c beside ∨i,→i,∃i\vee_i,\to_i,\exists_i). Derivability in the system reduces to intuitionistic derivability, yet Pereira, Pimentel and de Paiva argue that the ecumenical perspective cannot be reduced to the Gödel–Gentzen translation [6]. Why is the logical vocabulary divided exactly in this way, and what kind of unity holds the two logics together? The talk proposes a categorical answer.

Interpret formulas in the subobject lattices of an arbitrary topos ℰ\mathcal{E}, and let c=¬¬c=\neg\neg be the universal closure operation of the double-negation topology. The cc-closed subobjects of XX form a Boolean algebra Cl(X)\mathrm{Cl}(X), isomorphic to the subobject lattice of the associated sheaf of XX in Sh¬¬(ℰ)\mathrm{Sh}_{\neg\neg}(\mathcal{E}) [5], and the inclusion Cl(X)↪Sub(X)\mathrm{Cl}(X)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Sub}(X) is right adjoint to cc. The shared operators all preserve closed subobjects: substitution f*f^*, ∧\wedge, ∀f\forall_f, ⊥\bot, and ¬\neg (indeed ¬A\neg A is closed for every AA). Joins and ∃f\exists_f, which are left adjoints (∃f⊣f*\exists_f\dashv f^* in Lawvere’s analysis of the quantifiers [4]), do not, and must be recomputed by closure. Up to intuitionistic equivalence, the classical operators are exactly these recomputations: A∨cB=¬¬(A∨B),∃cxA=¬¬∃xA,A→cB=A→¬¬B.A\vee_c B=\neg\neg(A\vee B),\qquad \exists_c x\,A=\neg\neg\,\exists x\,A,\qquad A\to_c B=A\to\neg\neg B. In short, existence splits because it is a left adjoint; implication, itself a right adjoint, splits only through its consequent. For the single-atom formulation of [6], this semantics is sound and complete.

The reflection is oriented. Sh¬¬(ℰ)\mathrm{Sh}_{\neg\neg}(\mathcal{E}) is a dense Boolean subtopos of every topos, whereas every subtopos of a Boolean topos is Boolean [2]: the intuitionistic layer can never appear as a reflection of the classical one inside a single universe, and hosting it requires an added modality (the S4 translation recalled in [7]). On this reading, the ecumenical system is not a translation between two languages but a reflection internal to one topos, and the unit A≤¬¬AA\leq\neg\neg A measures the gap between the two existences. Since the argument uses only density, any dense Lawvere–Tierney topology jj yields the same pattern, with ∃j=j∃\exists_j=j\,\exists and A→jB=A→jBA\to_j B=A\to jB, which suggests a family of “jj-ecumenical” calculi related to Aczel’s lax modalities [1].

The facts involved are standard topos theory; the contribution is to read Prawitz’s division of the logical vocabulary as the trace of an adjunction. The talk ends with a short historical remark: this reading gives a precise content to Albert Lautman’s notion of a mixte (1938), a domain in which the structure of one theory is reflected within another [3].
